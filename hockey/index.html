Air Hockey World Model

There is no game engine here. A diffusion transformer trained on recorded matches imagines every frame from the keys you press, plays the red side itself, and runs entirely in your browser.

How this works

Each game frame pair is one latent: a 5 × 4 grid of 32 numbers per player. Every step the model denoises the next latent from noise, conditioned on the last 19 latents and on both players' keys, and a small decoder turns it into the two pictures you see. Red's keys are replaced by the model's learned "unknown" input, so the model plays that side itself.

  • Real time is 30 latents per second (60 frames per second). The model and the two views' decoders run on the CPU in three Web Workers, so a machine with four or more cores plays fastest.
  • The DiT is 36M parameters, quantized to 8 bits. The decoder's feed-forward layers are pruned by 30% with least-squares compensation.
  • Long rollouts drift from real physics, and the match timer can end early, the model's known failure.
